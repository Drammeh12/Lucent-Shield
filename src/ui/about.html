Security beyond boundaries.

Most security tools hand you a wall of jargon and leave you to figure out what it means. Lucent Shield does the opposite. It watches your websites and your code, tells you in plain words what is exposed, and spells out exactly how to fix it. Sites hosted on Bash Cloud are also shielded: attacks are stopped before they reach them.

What it does

White-box scanning

Point it at your own code and it looks for leaked secrets, risky patterns, and dependencies with known vulnerabilities.

Black-box scanning

Give it a website you own and it checks the outside view: headers, TLS, cookies, and files that should never be public.

Monitoring

Once you press Monitor, Lucent Shield checks again every few hours and alerts you the moment something gets worse, so you are never the last to know.

Forensics

Inspect a file or an artifact, capture its fingerprint, and surface anything that looks out of place.

Monitored, or shielded

We are careful about what we promise, so here is exactly what you get.

Any website or code: monitored

Lucent Shield finds the weaknesses, tells you how to fix each one, and keeps watching. Your site becomes safer when you or your developer apply the fixes. Monitoring does not change your site and does not block attackers.

Sites hosted on Bash Cloud: shielded

Every visit passes through Lucent Shield before it reaches your site. It blocks common attacks such as SQL injection, script injection and path traversal, never serves secret files like .env or .git, slows down anyone guessing passwords, hides your server version and adds the security headers your site is missing. You can watch every blocked attempt and show a live "Shielded by Lucent Shield" badge.

The shield is a strong first line, not a replacement for fixing your site: it checks the address of every visit, not the contents of forms, so keep your code up to date and use the scans to fix the causes. A green padlock means our checks found nothing serious at the last scan. No tool can promise that a site cannot be hacked, and we never will.
